# 7. IMPORTANT RESEARCH CONTEXT

The repository contains research material establishing the following:

### Classical TB/CXR research

Classical TB detection from chest X-rays is already an established research domain.

### Segmentation

The supplied 2020 TB study demonstrated that lung segmentation can materially affect downstream TB classification and reported stronger results for segmented-lung inputs than whole images in that study.

Therefore:

> **“We use lung segmentation” is NOT by itself our novelty claim.**

### QML

The supplied research indicates that QML in medical imaging remains an emerging area with inconsistent evidence of superiority over classical models.

Therefore:

> **“We use QML” is NOT by itself our novelty claim.**

### Candidate gaps

The supplied research identifies promising underexplored directions including:

* segmentation-guided QML;
* source-separated/external validation;
* low-data learning behaviour;
* calibration;
* uncertainty;
* resource-accounted benchmarking;
* lesion-aware representations;
* explainability.

These are candidate research areas, not guaranteed final contributions.

# 11. FAIR EXPERIMENTATION PRINCIPLE

The core experimental comparison must be fair.

Especially:

```text
Same image
Same preprocessing
Same segmentation
Same feature extractor
Same normalization
Same dimensionality reduction
Different downstream learner
```

For the first quantum comparison:

```text
PCA features
      ↓
RBF-SVM
```

versus

```text
PCA features
      ↓
QSVM
```

The quantum model must not receive a privileged representation.

The benchmark must record:

* AUROC
* AUPRC where meaningful
* F1
* sensitivity
* specificity
* balanced accuracy
* MCC where meaningful
* confusion matrix
* runtime
* feature dimension
* qubit count
* circuit depth
* shots / circuit evaluations where applicable

Do not fabricate results.
